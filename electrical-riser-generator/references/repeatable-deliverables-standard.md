# Repeatable electrical deliverables standard

This document is the controlling production standard for load calculations, panel schedules, feeder schedules, and riser diagrams generated by this repository.

## 1. Evidence hierarchy

Every value must be classified before calculations begin.

1. Equipment schedule or nameplate data.
2. Electrical plan notes and panel/riser information.
3. Mechanical, plumbing, fire-protection, lighting, and architectural plans.
4. Owner/user-provided equipment list or stated service size.
5. Explicit preliminary assumption.

Never silently replace a higher-priority source with a lower-priority assumption. Record conflicts in the assumptions register.

## 2. Required source register

Create a project source register with these fields:

- Item or load identifier.
- Source sheet/file and location.
- Voltage, phase, poles, FLA/MCA/MOCP, watts, VA, or kVA.
- Quantity.
- Serving panel/location.
- Status: `PLAN`, `SCHEDULE`, `OWNER`, `ASSUMED`, or `FIELD VERIFY`.
- Notes and conflicts.

No load may enter the final calculation unless it appears in the source register.

## 3. Load-calculation rules

### 3.1 Separate ratings from calculated loads

Keep these concepts distinct:

- Existing service rating.
- Panel bus/main rating.
- Feeder/OCP rating.
- Connected load.
- Demand load.
- Calculated current.

The sum of downstream panel ratings is not the service load. A 400A service remains 400A even when downstream feeder ratings total more than 400A; service adequacy is evaluated from the calculated demand and existing loads.

### 3.2 Calculate from actual equipment data first

Use actual fixture wattages, quantities, and equipment MCA/MOCP or nameplate values where available. Do not substitute generic branch-circuit capacity for actual load unless the equipment rating is unknown.

When only a branch-circuit recommendation is provided, label the load as an allowance. Example: `20A, 120V branch allowance = 2.40 kVA`.

### 3.3 Prevent duplicate loads

Before totaling, compare owner equipment lists against mechanical schedules and plan callouts. Generic entries such as `split A/C compressor` or `hood exhaust fan` must not be added again if the scheduled ACCU or exhaust fan is the same equipment.

Each equipment item must have one canonical load ID. Duplicates must be resolved or flagged.

### 3.4 Motor and HVAC basis

- Use MCA for feeder/load calculations when supplied.
- Use MOCP only for OCP selection, not as connected load.
- Apply required largest-motor adder only once at the applicable feeder/service level.
- Keep noncoincident heating/cooling logic explicit.
- FCU, ERV, EF, ACCU, RTU, pumps, and refrigeration equipment remain separate load blocks unless documentation proves they are integral to one listed assembly.

### 3.5 Continuous loads

Identify continuous loads and apply the required factor explicitly. Do not multiply every circuit by 125% by default. Record the code basis and which rows receive the factor.

### 3.6 Phase balancing

For 120/208V, 3-phase, 4-wire panels:

- Allocate 1-pole loads across A, B, and C.
- Allocate 2-pole loads across AB, BC, and CA in rotation.
- Allocate 3-pole loads equally to all phases.
- Report phase VA and phase current.
- Flag phase imbalance above the project threshold, default 10%.

### 3.7 Service check

Report:

- Total connected kVA.
- Total demand kVA.
- Calculated demand current.
- Existing service capacity in kVA.
- Percent utilization.
- Remaining capacity.

If existing building loads are unknown, state that the reported service cannot be declared adequate without field demand data or an approved existing-load method.

## 4. Panel schedule standard

Use the compact blue-header format.

### 4.1 Header block

Include:

- Panel name and description.
- Location.
- Mains/bus rating.
- Voltage, phase, and wire count.
- Mounting.
- AIC/SCCR status.
- Source feeder and feeder ID.

### 4.2 Circuit table

Column order:

`CKT | DESCRIPTION | BREAKER | A | B | C | BREAKER | DESCRIPTION | CKT`

Rules:

- Phase columns contain `X` marks only.
- 2-pole and 3-pole breakers occupy consecutive circuit positions.
- Continuation rows contain `(description cont.)` and no repeated breaker rating.
- Do not collapse a multi-pole load into one row.
- Split lighting and general receptacles into multiple circuits when needed.
- Preserve exact plan equipment names where available.
- Label assumptions and field-verification items in schedule notes.

### 4.3 Panel rating logic

Panel rating is a design selection, not automatically equal to calculated load current. Select a standard rating with reasonable spare capacity, then verify that bus, main, feeder, OCP, and conductor sizing all agree.

## 5. Feeder schedule standard

Minimum columns:

`ID | Source | Destination | Rating | OCP/Disconnect | Conductors | EGC | Raceway | Load basis | Notes`

Rules:

- Every riser feeder label must have exactly one feeder-table row.
- Every feeder-table row must appear on the riser.
- Do not size conductors from panel rating alone when the feeder has special tap, transformer, motor, or fire-pump rules.
- Preliminary copper/75C/EMT assumptions must be stated.
- Transformer primary and secondary feeders must be independently calculated.

## 6. Riser diagram standard

### 6.1 Geometry and symbols

- Panel boxes show ratings only; names are outside boxes.
- Feeder IDs and ratings sit directly beside conductor runs.
- Fused disconnect symbols are inline and connected at both ends.
- Use the switch-fuse-disconnector symbol consistently in the legend and diagram.
- No floating disconnects, open-ended feeders, or duplicate remote panels.
- Remote panels appear only at their actual serving location.
- Service/electrical rooms show feeder disconnects and callouts, not duplicate panel boxes.

### 6.2 Topology

- Branch loads connect in parallel from a source bus.
- Never daisy-chain unrelated downstream panels or equipment.
- Transformer workflows show source panel/bus -> primary OCP/disconnect -> transformer -> secondary protection/tap arrangement -> destination panels.
- Any transformer not shown in the source plans must be labeled `ASSUMED - FIELD VERIFY`.
- Do not invent an intermediate distribution panel solely for drafting convenience.

### 6.3 Editable deliverables

The `.drawio` file must contain native editable `mxCell` vertices and edges for every line, box, label, bus, and symbol. An embedded SVG, image background, XML comment, or single grouped image is not an acceptable editable deliverable.

The `.drawio`, SVG, and PDF must depict the same topology, labels, ratings, and geometry.

## 7. Mandatory QA gates

Before issue, validate all of the following:

- Source register complete.
- No duplicate canonical load IDs.
- Connected and demand totals reconcile.
- Service, panel, feeder, and OCP ratings are not conflated.
- Panel ratings match riser and feeder schedule.
- Feeder IDs are one-to-one across riser and feeder table.
- Multi-pole panel rows are consecutive and phase-correct.
- Phase balance is reported.
- Transformer assumptions are labeled.
- No invented panels remain.
- All lines and symbols are visibly connected.
- Drawio is natively editable and visually matches SVG/PDF.
- DOCX and PDF outputs are rendered and visually inspected.
- Spreadsheet formulas recalculate without errors.

## 8. Required deliverable package

Unless the user narrows the scope, issue:

- Load calculation DOCX and PDF.
- Panel schedules DOCX and PDF.
- Riser plus feeder schedule PDF.
- Native editable `.drawio`.
- Editable SVG.
- Feeder schedule XLSX and CSV.
- Assumptions/source register.
- ZIP containing the complete coordinated package.

All files must use a common project name and revision.
